# Opening: Prosecution

**1. It adds a layer on top of a base that has never run.** The record says: "Detection, RL training and the SUMO experiments have not run yet because SUMO, Gymnasium and Stable-Baselines3 are not installed, and no footage has been recorded." In the console, the "RL agent" and forecaster "are currently stand-ins" and the simulator is "a small built-in microsimulation standing in for SUMO." An explanation layer can only explain decisions that exist. Cost: effort goes into explaining a pressure rule. If the core results slip, the project ends up with a polished "why" layer sitting on decisions no trained agent ever made.

**2. The pitch claims something the design cannot do.** "Can prove why it made every decision." The evidence record holds "the queues the camera measured, the forecast, the agent's choice, and which safety-shield rule fired." That records what went into a PPO/DQN policy and what came out. It does not record why the policy chose what it did. Shield rules are causal and can be logged, but the learned policy's reasons are not in any record. Cost: an examiner asks "how is this proof of why?" and the headline claim falls apart.

**3. The verifier checks that answers match the logs, not that they are true.** "Each number must match the logged data and each claim must cite a record." If the camera's counts are wrong, a wrong answer still passes as long as it repeats the log. A "why" claim can cite a real record and still give a false reason. The record does not say how the verifier would check claims that are not numbers. The evaluation counts how often it catches "deliberately injected wrong answers", which the author writes himself, so the test measures errors he designed. Cost: the headline verifier figure is circular.

**4. It spreads a final-year project across one more field.** The stated bar is to "show competency in Computing principles and Fundamentals." The project already covers computer vision, time-series forecasting, safe RL and simulation calibration. The proposal adds LLM agents, with no stated model, cost, offline plan or ground-truth labelling effort; the record is silent on all of these. The idea comes from a hackathon pattern ("Every decision cites its evidence"), not from a gap in Makutano. Cost: each module ends up shallower, and the examiners see a bolt-on that follows a trend.
